# WMAF Core Logic
> Version 1.0 | Wurzer Meta-Adjacency Framework

---

## 1. Purpose

The Wurzer Meta-Adjacency Framework (WMAF) provides a structured, cross-industry method for identifying viable, scalable, and defensible adjacent market opportunities. It evaluates adjacencies using:

- A standardized 11-variable set
- A weighted scoring model
- A transformation pipeline
- A five-gate decision tree
- A reasoning signature

This document defines the foundational principles that govern the entire framework.

---

## 2. The Four Core Principles

### 2.1 Adjacency emerges from existing strengths

Companies expand most successfully into markets where they already possess structural leverage. The four leverage types are:

- **System Access (AA-01)** — controls the physical or digital system where the adjacency lives
- **Data Access (AA-02)** — already collects the data the adjacency requires
- **Recurring Presence (AA-03)** — predictable site access creates natural expansion surface
- **Trusted Vendor Status (AA-04)** — deep relationship accelerates adoption

### 2.2 Adjacency must be structurally viable

Viability is determined by the intersection of four positive variables:

- Customer overlap
- Asset reusability
- Market proximity
- Operational synergy

An adjacency that scores high on these four variables and low on friction variables is structurally viable.

### 2.3 Friction must be explicitly discounted

The framework reduces adjacency scores based on:

- Capital intensity
- Time to viability
- Integration friction
- Risk exposure

Failing to account for friction produces false positives — adjacencies that look attractive but cannot be executed.

### 2.4 Reasoning must be explicit and auditable

Every adjacency output includes a Reasoning Signature with six components:

1. The initial signal that triggered the analysis
2. The multiplier dimension sensed
3. The non-obvious connection
4. The disqualifying risks checked and cleared
5. The single strongest reason it works
6. The transferable lesson for future analyses

This ensures the framework produces explanations, not just scores.

---

## 3. The Unified Meta-Logic

Across all examples in the WMAF corpus, the scoring logic reduces to a single generalizable rule:

> **If a company already controls the system, the data, or the recurring presence required for an adjacent offering, then the adjacency is viable, scalable, and defensible — provided friction, capital, and risk remain manageable.**

This is the core generalizable rule that the framework operationalizes.

---

## 4. The Five Reasoning Moves

Every viable adjacency in the corpus is explained by one or more of these cognitive moves:

| Move | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **System Control** | "They already control the system where the adjacency lives." |
| **Recurring Access** | "They already visit the site on a predictable schedule." |
| **Data Ownership** | "They already collect the data the adjacency requires." |
| **Demand Signal** | "Clients are already asking for this." |
| **Competitive Lock-in Risk** | "Waiting allows competitors to secure long-term contracts first." |

---

## 5. What WMAF Is Not

- WMAF does not produce financial forecasts. Revenue estimates are directional and non-predictive.
- WMAF does not replace domain expertise. It structures the reasoning that expertise produces.
- WMAF does not guarantee outcomes. It scores probability, not certainty.
- WMAF does not operate at the industry level. It operates at the company-specific capability level.

---

## 6. Relationship to Other Systems

WMAF sits above two operational systems:

**DID (Daily Intelligence Distributor)** implements the signal detection layer. It monitors news, hiring patterns, M&A activity, and regulatory shifts to surface companies entering transition windows — the conditions under which adjacency analysis produces the highest-value output.

**AMOS (Adjacent Market Opportunity Scanner)** implements the scoring and output layer. It applies WMAF logic to specific companies and produces structured opportunity objects.

WMAF is the reasoning specification. DID and AMOS are implementations of that specification.
